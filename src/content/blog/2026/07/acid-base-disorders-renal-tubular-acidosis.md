Renal tubular acidosis (RTA) is characterized by a hyperchloremic normal anion gap metabolic acidosis due to renal tubular defects in acid-base handling.

## Comparing RTA Types

### Type 1 (Distal RTA)
- **Defect:** Impaired distal H+ secretion.
- **Urinary pH:** > 5.5 (inability to acidify urine).
- **Serum Potassium:** Hypokalemia.
- **Key Association:** Nephrolithiasis (due to hypercalciuria).

### Type 2 (Proximal RTA)
- **Defect:** Impaired proximal HCO3- reabsorption.
- **Urinary pH:** < 5.5 (distal acidification intact).
- **Serum Potassium:** Hypokalemia.
- **Key Association:** Fanconi syndrome.

### Type 4 (Hyperkalemic RTA)
- **Defect:** Hypoaldosteronism or aldosterone resistance.
- **Urinary pH:** < 5.5.
- **Serum Potassium:** Hyperkalemia (defining feature).
