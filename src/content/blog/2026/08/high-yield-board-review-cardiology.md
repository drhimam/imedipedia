Prepare for the cardiology boards with these high-yield practice scenarios and explanations.

## Question 1: Valvular Pathology
A 70-year-old male presents with exertional syncope, dyspnea, and angina. On examination, a harsh cresc-decresc systolic murmur is heard at the second right intercostal space, radiating to the carotids. What is the most appropriate next step in management?

- **Explanation:** This clinical triad (Syncope, Angina, Dyspnea) points directly to severe Aortic Stenosis. The diagnostic gold standard and next best step is an **Echocardiogram** to assess valve area and transvalvular gradients.

## Question 2: Arrhythmia Management
A 65-year-old female presents with palpitations and fatigue. ECG shows irregular rhythm with absent P waves. The patient is hemodynamically stable. What is the priority treatment?

- **Explanation:** The ECG features are pathognomonic for **Atrial Fibrillation**. In a hemodynamically stable patient, the primary goals are rate control (e.g., beta-blockers) and stroke prevention assessment using the CHA2DS2-VASc score.
