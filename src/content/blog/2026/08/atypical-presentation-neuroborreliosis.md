Bilateral facial nerve palsy is a clinical rarity, occurring in less than 2% of facial palsy cases. It requires urgent diagnostic workup as it is often linked to systemic conditions.

## Case Description
A 29-year-old female presented with progressive weakness in both sides of her face, resulting in difficulty closing her eyes and an inability to smile. She recalled a camping trip in New England 4 weeks prior but did not note any erythema migrans rash.

## Evaluation and Findings
- **Physical Examination:** Bilateral weakness in all facial muscle groups (House-Brackmann grade IV).
- **Lumbar Puncture:** CSF analysis showed lymphocytic pleocytosis and elevated protein.
- **Serology:** CSF and serum ELISA/Western Blot confirmed positive antibodies for *Borrelia burgdorferi*.

## Treatment and Resolution
The patient was prescribed a 21-day course of intravenous ceftriaxone, resulting in a full recovery of facial symmetry over the next three months.
